# irori 0.1.44 — Your AI, and moving between the Overview and a brain

This preview completes the new design (v5). The Overview gains **your AI**
(あなたの AI): your own agent across the brains of a workspace. It hands each part
of a request to that brain's AI, which reads the brain's own Schema first, and
tells you what each did. Moving between the Overview and a brain now zooms.

This preview carries three steps. 0.1.42 and 0.1.43 were prepared but not
published on their own; their notes are kept in the repository
([0.1.42](https://github.com/DeL-TaiseiOzaki/irori/blob/main/docs/releases/0.1.42-preview.1.md),
[0.1.43](https://github.com/DeL-TaiseiOzaki/irori/blob/main/docs/releases/0.1.43-preview.1.md)).

## Your AI

- **Set it up once.** Open **全体** (Overview); beside the map, **あなたの AI** offers
  **あなたの AI を用意する**. irori creates your AI's folder, `~/irori/you` on this
  device, with a short `AGENTS.md` and a `brain-agents` skill. It never writes
  over files that are already there.
- **Each brain gets its own sub-agent.** Your AI keeps a definition for each
  brain in its folder (`.claude/agents/<brain>.md`). The definition tells the
  sub-agent to read that brain's `AGENTS.md` and skills, work only inside that
  brain, and report what it changed. Your AI writes these definitions itself
  when you ask: open its folder from the orb on the map (**あなたの Schema**) and
  press **あなたの AI に定義を更新させる**.
- **See the hand-offs.** Your AI's replies show a task list: which brain got
  what, and whether it is working, waiting for your permission, or done. Each
  brain's report follows. On the map, a line with a moving spark runs from
  your AI to each brain it is working with.
- **Answer from the Overview.** When a brain's sub-agent wants to change a file,
  the request appears in your AI's panel, marked with the brain it comes from.
- **Brains stay in their lanes.** irori lets your AI write only in its own
  folder, and each brain's sub-agent only inside its own brain. Work that
  needs a brain goes through that brain's sub-agent. While your AI works, the
  brains it was given wait: their own AI, Git and settings resume when it
  finishes.
- Your AI runs on **Claude Code** for now, with its standard permission mode.
  Codex follows later.

## Moving between the Overview and a brain

- **Zoom between levels.** Opening a brain from the Overview's map zooms in: the
  map grows away around the brain you chose while the brain's page settles in.
  Going back to the Overview zooms out.
- **Sheets and requests.** Sheets such as Brain settings and the Drive
  connection rise into place over a fading backdrop. A permission request from
  an AI arrives with a small spring before it starts to breathe.
- **Less motion.** When your system asks for reduced motion, the levels switch
  at once and every loop — rings, glows, sparks, the floating map — stays still.
- **Finish.**
  - Schema files keep their names in the brain's tree (`AGENTS.md`), while notes
    still read as titles.
  - The brain's home lists the instruction files first, and its crumb shows a
    house.
  - A small graph appears at its own size instead of filling the page.
  - The Overview's buttons and your AI's header match the design.

## Fixed

- A permission request could appear as already ended when Claude Code reported
  the tool call after asking. That affected ordinary brain runs too. Requests
  now end only when they are answered, declined or cancelled.
- Opening the backlinks list while the count on the link button was refreshing
  could show 「新しい検索に切り替わりました」 instead of the list. The two now share
  one look at the KB.
- While you resolve a merge conflict in **変更** (Changes), a change to the same
  file from outside irori makes **統合内容を保存して解決** refuse once and ask you to
  look again, as intended. The next attempt could be refused as well, until
  irori noticed the change on its own. irori now reads the file again as soon
  as it refuses. Your merged text is kept either way.

## Installing

**Windows 11 x64:** download `irori-0.1.44-windows-x64-Setup.exe` and run it. The
installer carries no distribution signature, so Windows shows a warning that the
publisher cannot be verified.

**Mac (Apple silicon):** download `irori-0.1.44-macos-arm64.dmg`, open it, and
drag **irori** into **アプリケーション**. Intel Macs are not supported. The first
launch is refused because the package carries no distribution signature. Open
**システム設定 → プライバシーとセキュリティ**, find irori's blocked notice under
セキュリティ, and press **このまま開く**. Later launches behave normally. Keep
irori in **アプリケーション**: the in-app update replaces the app where it is.

An installed 0.1.29 or later offers this version with **更新して再起動**.

## Verification

Every step ran the full build, unit tests and Electron UI suites.

- **Your AI**, through a Claude Code protocol fixture, in unit tests and in the
  app:
  - setup;
  - a hand-off answered from the Overview, and its report;
  - the brains held while it works;
  - your AI's own write in a brain refused;
  - hand-offs kept in the foreground;
  - definitions written on request.
- **Real Claude Code** (2.1.280) confirmed the parts only a model can: the
  definitions load from your AI's folder, the sub-agent follows its brain's
  `AGENTS.md`, the write rule holds for a sub-agent and for your AI, and a
  resumed conversation still delegates.
- **The zoom:** the Overview test checks it, and checks that nothing zooms
  under reduced motion.
- **Screens** were compared with the design canvas in the hearth, dark and light
  themes.

## Remaining boundaries

Passing CI is not acceptance on an installed device.

- A brain's sub-agent works from your AI's folder and settings. It reaches the
  brain through its definition, so the brain's own Claude Code settings do not
  apply to it.
- Terminal commands are not bounded by brain; Claude Code asks you for them as
  usual.
- Every free brain of the workspace waits for the whole of your AI's request.
- Your AI runs on Claude Code; Codex comes later.
